    How Naltrexone Gum Works for Patients in Conway, SC

    How Naltrexone Gum Works in Conway

    Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors, and the 50 mg tablet has been FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder for decades. A compounding pharmacy mixes a set amount of naltrexone into each piece of gum. Some of the drug may be absorbed through the lining of the mouth while you chew, and the rest is swallowed with saliva. That is the idea. Nobody has shown the gum works better than a tablet.

    Who Naltrexone Gum Is For

    Adults who would be reasonable candidates for naltrexone but dislike swallowing pills are the main group. Some ask about it as a low dose wellness option. Others are working on cravings and want something they can chew right before a known trigger, like a Friday evening or a long drive home. Opioid users are not candidates. Significant liver disease usually points to a different plan.

    Dosing and Use in Conway

    Your provider sets the strength of each piece and how often to use it. Most plans have you chew slowly for a set time, often around 20 to 30 minutes, then throw the piece away. One piece a day is common. Targeted plans use a piece shortly before a known trigger, with a written cap on how many pieces a day. Compounded strengths vary, so follow the label exactly.

    What to Expect in the First Weeks

    The first week is mostly about learning the routine. Some people get mild nausea or a headache when they start any form of naltrexone, and a few notice lighter sleep for several nights. Most of it settles by week two. The taste takes some getting used to. At a check-in around week two, your provider asks how many pieces you are using, whether the timing works and whether your gums feel sore.

    Side Effects of Naltrexone Gum in Conway

    The side effects match other forms of naltrexone and include nausea, headache, dizziness and trouble sleeping. Chewing brings a couple of extras, such as a bitter aftertaste and sore gums. Opioids are the serious concern. Anyone who takes opioids, even now and then, can go into withdrawal on naltrexone, and opioid pain pills may stop working. Yellow eyes, dark urine or bad upper belly pain need a same-day message.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your questionnaire asks about current medications, opioid use, liver health, pregnancy and breastfeeding, plus any major medical conditions. Two drugs get named directly, tramadol and codeine syrup. A recent liver panel showing ALT and AST may be requested when your history raises a flag. Jaw trouble matters too. People with jaw pain or recent dental work are often pointed to capsules instead.

    How Naltrexone Gum Compares in Conway

    Standard naltrexone tablets have decades of research and FDA approval for alcohol and opioid use disorder. LDN capsules sit at the other end, compounded and off-label at a few milligrams a night. The gum is a newer compounded format with limited published data, chosen mostly for convenience or because swallowing pills is a problem. Weight loss is a different story. GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide have much better weight loss data than naltrexone in any form.

    How the Online Process Works

    Start with a short online questionnaire about your health, goals and medications. Within about a business day, a licensed provider reads your intake and often messages a question or two, most often about pain medicine or past liver labs. If the gum fits, the prescription goes to a licensed US compounding pharmacy. It ships to your home. Your label lists strength, chewing time and a daily maximum.
